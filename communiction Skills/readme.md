Day 2:

Walk to the center, stand still for 2 seconds, look at the audience, and say:

“Imagine two students graduating in the same year.
One is afraid that AI will replace his job.
The other learns how to use AI and becomes twice as productive.
Five years later, who do you think has more opportunities?”

Pause for 2 seconds.

Then say:

“Good morning everyone. Today I want to discuss a question that is worrying students, employees and companies around the world: Will AI take away our jobs, or will it create new opportunities?”

That is your hook.

S — Situation

“Artificial Intelligence is already becoming part of our daily life. We use it in ChatGPT, Google search, recommendation systems, banking, healthcare, education and even software development.

Companies are now using AI to complete work that earlier required many hours.”

Body language: Keep your hands open at waist/chest level. Look at the left, center and right sides of the audience instead of staring at one person.

Then introduce a small story:

“Let us imagine a person named Rahul. Rahul works in an office where he prepares reports, replies to emails and enters data manually.”

C — Challenge

Slightly slow your voice here.

“One day Rahul's company introduces AI.

AI can prepare reports in minutes, draft emails and analyze large amounts of data.

Rahul becomes worried.

He thinks, ‘If AI can do my work faster than me, will I still have a job?’”

Pause.

“And this is the real challenge. AI can automate repetitive tasks. Some jobs may change, some roles may reduce, and workers who refuse to learn new skills can face difficulties.”

Now present the other side:

“But there is another problem. AI cannot simply run every organization by itself. Someone still needs to understand the problem, communicate with people, make decisions, verify AI's output and use the technology correctly.”

Body language: When saying “AI can automate repetitive tasks,” gesture slightly to one side. When saying “But there is another side,” physically shift one small step to the other side. This visually shows the contrast.

A — Action

Now your story changes.

“Instead of fighting AI, Rahul decides to learn how to work with it.

He learns how to use AI for research, data analysis and report preparation.

Earlier, one report took him three hours. Now AI helps him prepare the first draft quickly, and Rahul spends his time checking the information, improving the analysis and making better decisions.”

Then broaden it:

“This is what we should do as students as well.

We should not only ask, ‘What can AI do?’
We should ask, ‘What can I do better with AI?’”

That line is excellent for your presentation. Pause after it.

You can add examples:

“A programmer can use AI to code faster.
A designer can generate ideas faster.
A doctor can use AI tools to support diagnosis.
A teacher can create personalized learning material.
An entrepreneur can use AI to understand customers and automate repetitive work.”

L — Learning

This is your main message.

“Rahul learns an important lesson: technology normally changes jobs before it completely removes jobs.

The real competition may not always be between humans and AI.

In many cases, it may be between a person who knows how to use AI and a person who does not.”

Pause. Look around the room.

Then say:

“So technical knowledge alone will not be enough. We also need creativity, communication, critical thinking, problem solving, adaptability and the ability to work with technology.”

This is a strong point because it doesn't blindly say AI is good or bad.

T — Transformation

Now finish the story.

“Six months later, Rahul is no longer afraid of AI.

Instead of spending his entire day doing repetitive work, he uses AI to automate part of it and spends more time solving important problems.

His job did not simply disappear. His role changed.”

Then transform the story into your conclusion:

“The same transformation can happen to us.

AI will definitely change the job market. Some repetitive tasks may disappear, and some existing jobs will be redesigned. But at the same time, new roles, businesses and opportunities will also emerge.”

Strong closing

Move back to the center. Slow down.

“So, will AI take away jobs or create opportunities?

I believe the better question is:

Are we preparing ourselves for the jobs AI is going to change and create?”

Pause.

Then finish:

“We cannot stop technology from changing.
But we can decide whether we fear that change or learn to use it.

AI should not only be seen as our competitor.
If we learn to use it wisely, it can become one of our most powerful tools.

Thank you.”
